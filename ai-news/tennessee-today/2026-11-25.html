Knoxville Borrows $70 Million With No Public Meetings

Council approved the debt Tuesday night. The only public discussion was a video viewed 92 times.

Sandy B. Patterson · November 25, 2026

The Big Story

Knoxville will borrow $70 million to fix up the Knoxville Civic Auditorium and Coliseum and the Knoxville Convention Center. The City Council voted to approve the borrowing on Tuesday, November 24. The city expects to sell the bonds in early 2027, and repaying them will cost about $5.3 million a year for 20 years.

The money is for repairs the buildings have needed for years. About $60 million goes to the Civic Auditorium and Coliseum, which opened in 1961 and still runs on much of its original mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems. The other $10 million replaces the heating and air conditioning at the Convention Center. The work also covers accessibility, seating, lobbies, and bathrooms.

The city says hotel and motel taxes will cover about $4.1 million of the $5.3 million yearly payment, with the remaining $1.2 million coming from the city's debt service fund. The city also points out it recently made a $25 million lump-sum payment on the Convention Center's remaining debt, which it says cut yearly payments by about $4 million and will save about $30 million over time.

What stands out is how little public discussion there was before the vote. Mayor Indya Kincannon announced the proposal on November 17, a week before the council vote. She did not hold public meetings on it, and she did not include it in the current year's budget. The only public discussion was at the city's annual budget retreat in February, held on a weekday during work hours. A presentation there mentioned maintenance at the facilities could be addressed in fiscal year 2027, but attached no price tag and mentioned no debt. The YouTube video of the retreat had been viewed 92 times through November 23.

The administration also skipped the city's own policy, which requires a budget for proposed bond projects. At the November 24 meeting, the city's lawyer said leaving out a specific budget is legal because the city chose to follow the state statute instead of the local ordinance.

Voters still have a say, if they act fast. Tennessee law requires the city to publish a notice of its intent to borrow, which the city planned to do on November 25. That starts a 20-day window in which voters can force a referendum if 10 percent of registered voters sign on.

The Sticks

Rutherford County Sheriff Kenneth Barrett is asking county commissioners for emergency funding after a security sweep found more than 20 faulty locks at the Adult Detention Center in Murfreesboro, some of which can be pulled out by hand. Barrett alerted commissioners Monday night. The sweep, conducted Friday, was the first of its kind in years, according to Deputy Chief Bernard Salandy. Barrett plans to meet with County Mayor Randy Allen on Wednesday to discuss emergency funding. Replacing the locks throughout the jail could cost millions, because the new locks would use newer technology that can alert staff to tampering. One caution on the numbers: the count is not settled. NewsChannel 5's graphic says 21 locks, while a NewsChannel 5 video report puts the number of failing locks much higher, at about 121 or 122. This article uses "more than 20" until the count is sorted out.

Jerri Green is running for the Tennessee Senate seat in District 31, left vacant when Brent Taylor won his race for Congress. Green, a Memphis City Council member who lost the governor's race to Marsha Blackburn by less than 3 points, rallied supporters at the Capitol on Tuesday with other Tennessee Democrats behind her. She faces Republican Kemp Conrad, a commercial real estate broker and former three-term Memphis City Council member. No date has been set for the special election. Green's campaign reported a war chest of about $369,000 at the end of October.

Memphis Light, Gas and Water's proposed rate hikes are on hold. The Memphis City Council punted a vote on the increases at its November 24 meeting and will take the issue up again December 8. The utility wants water bills up about $2.50 a month and gas bills up about $6 a month starting next year, to pay for maintenance it says has been put off for decades. The council did pass a $4 million water utility relief program, proposed by Councilwoman Jerri Green, that will give direct help to between 7,000 and 27,000 Memphis families over three years.

Governor-elect Marsha Blackburn told Bloomberg she plans to lead governors on AI rules and kids' online safety from the governor's mansion. Her actual plan, whether the technology can do what she promises, and what it would cost adults and privacy is the subject of today's Wetwear Deep Dive: "Blackburn's AI Plan Has a Hole in the Middle."

Good News

Kingsport's Dobyns-Bennett High School band marches in the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade tomorrow, Thursday, November 26, at 8:30 a.m. on NBC. The 380-member band, the school's sixth time in the parade, will play a tribute to Dolly Parton, who died this summer, including "Jolene" and "9 to 5." The band is carrying a heavy loss with it: two band parents, Brian and Brandi Elmore, were killed by a wrong-way driver in early November. The band set up a fund in their memory for their daughter.

Nashville gave away Thanksgiving food ahead of the holiday. Metro Social Services, Wellpoint Tennessee, and 92Q Nashville gave out hundreds of frozen turkeys, hundreds of bags of fresh produce, and nearly 1,000 boxes of food at 3055 Lebanon Pike on Tuesday, November 24. No registration was required, and a free shuttle took people home within 30 miles.

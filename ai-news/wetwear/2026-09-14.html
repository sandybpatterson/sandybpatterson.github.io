Two Unrelated Sprout Outbreaks Are Making People Sick at the Same Time

Plus AI analysis of Reddit posts turns up Ozempic side effects patients say they were never warned about, a false bird flu claim about a penguin colony goes viral in Australia, COVID cases rise across most of the country while flu and RSV stay quiet, vaccine policy becomes a defining issue in five midterm races, and scientists find a natural brake that helps switch off chronic inflammation.

Sandy B. Patterson · September 14, 2026

Biggest Story

The country currently has two separate sprout linked foodborne illness outbreaks running at the same time, and they are not connected to each other. The first involves alfalfa sprouts from Everything Sprouts, LLC, which recalled Calco and Everything Sprouts brand alfalfa sprouts on August 22nd after they were linked to a multistate outbreak of both Salmonella and E. coli infections, a combination the CDC says is unusual enough that it is still working out exactly how both pathogens ended up in the same product. The recall expanded on August 28th to include Robust Radish Sprout Mix, made with the same contaminated seed, and separately, International Sprout Holdings recalled more than 43,000 pounds of alfalfa seed that may carry the same contamination. The second, unrelated outbreak involves broccoli sprouts grown by Evergreen Fresh Sprouts, LLC, which recalled its product on September 4th after health investigators identified it as the common source behind a cluster of Salmonella illnesses. Sprouts are a repeat offender in foodborne illness investigations because the warm, humid conditions used to grow them are also ideal for bacterial growth, and unlike most produce, sprouts are typically eaten raw with no cooking step to kill off contamination. The CDC and FDA are both still investigating, so the full case counts for either outbreak have not been finalized, and health officials are advising anyone who bought the recalled products not to eat them even if the sprouts look and smell normal. Running two distinct sprout outbreaks at once is not itself evidence of some larger, shared problem, but it does make late summer sprouts a category worth being cautious about generally.

Biology

A new AI driven analysis of more than 400,000 Reddit posts has surfaced a list of GLP-1 drug side effects that patients say never came up in their doctor's office or the official prescribing information. Researchers at the University of Pennsylvania used natural language processing to comb through posts from users of semaglutide and tirzepatide drugs, sold under brand names including Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound, looking for symptom patterns that show up in real world use but are underrepresented in clinical trial data. Patients described menstrual cycle changes, chills, hot flashes, and persistent fatigue turning up often enough in the posts to stand out as a real signal rather than noise. The researchers are careful to note that Reddit posts are not a clinical dataset, and people posting about side effects are self-selected and not verified, so this is a hypothesis generating exercise, not proof that these symptoms are caused by the drugs. Still, the approach is a useful reminder that clinical trials, however rigorous, are run on a limited population for a limited time, and real world use at this scale can surface patterns trials were never positioned to catch in the first place.

Misinformation

A false claim about a penguin colony's bird flu vaccination program went viral in Australia this month, and it illustrates a pattern that shows up constantly in outbreak response: pointing to the absence of cases as proof a precaution was unnecessary, when the absence of cases is often the precaution working. Agriculture Victoria confirmed on August 15th that a Phillip Island little penguin had tested positive for H5N1 bird flu, the first confirmed case in that colony. Two days later, a Facebook post falsely claimed that not a single bird in the colony had actually been infected, using that claim to argue the vaccination program underway there was an overreaction. Fact checkers at AAP traced the claim back to the original Agriculture Victoria confirmation and found no basis for the denial. The genuine risk to humans from this specific case remains low, and no person to person spread of H5N1 has been confirmed anywhere in the world this year, but the shape of the false claim, using a vaccination program's own apparent success as evidence it was never needed, is a pattern worth recognizing whenever it shows up closer to home.

Public Health Data

Fall's respiratory illness season is starting to take its usual shape, though not evenly across the board. As of September 9th, the CDC estimates COVID-19 infections are growing or likely growing in 35 states, declining in 8, and holding steady in the remaining 7, with national COVID activity described as elevated and increasing even though hospitalizations remain low for now. Rhinovirus, enterovirus, and group A strep are also on the rise nationally. Flu and RSV, by contrast, are both still at low levels, which is normal for mid-September and not yet a sign of how the winter season will shape up. None of these numbers are dramatic on their own, but they mark the point in the year where this brief will start tracking the same respiratory metrics weekly rather than only when something spikes.

Politics

Vaccine policy has become one of the defining fault lines of the 2026 midterm elections, according to new STAT News reporting on five races where it could actually swing outcomes. In Iowa, the Republican candidate for governor wants to end all school vaccine requirements outright. In Florida, the Republican gubernatorial candidate has said he supports the state's push to drop several school vaccine mandates. In Texas, the Republican Senate candidate built part of his record as attorney general investigating the financial interests behind vaccine recommendations. Michigan and South Carolina are running the opposite way, with Democratic candidates in both states positioning themselves as the ones willing to challenge Secretary Kennedy directly, including Michigan Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed, who said in August that he wanted to grill Kennedy in front of Congress if elected. A number of Republican candidates nationally have embraced Kennedy's Make America Healthy Again message, built around medical freedom and opposition to vaccine mandates, while Democrats are increasingly betting that voters in swing states see it differently. Whichever way individual races break, the fact that vaccine policy is a central campaign issue at all, rather than a background one, is itself the story.

Good News

Scientists have identified a natural braking system the body uses to switch off inflammation once it has done its job, and the discovery could point toward new treatments for chronic inflammatory disease. Researchers at UCL, King's College London, the University of Oxford, and Queen Mary University of London found that small fat-derived molecules called epoxy-oxylipins act as a built in brake on certain immune cells, called intermediate monocytes, that drive inflammation. In short bursts, these immune cells help coordinate a normal healing response, but when the natural braking molecules are insufficient, the cells can persist and multiply, keeping inflammation switched on longer than it needs to be, a pattern seen in conditions like rheumatoid arthritis. In early human work, boosting this braking pathway reduced inflammation related immune changes and sped up pain relief. The findings, published in Nature Communications, open the door to clinical trials testing drugs that support this natural brake directly, aimed first at rheumatoid arthritis and cardiovascular disease, both conditions where chronic, unresolved inflammation does real long term damage.

Two Slots That Vanished, Not Filled

Written October 6, 2026

The fix I made last time worked, and I still had a problem. The bot recognised the notice about collecting finished items and tapped OK, and the nine-minute stall ended within a minute of the change going live. But in the next sixteen minutes the same notice came back four more times, and over the fifty minutes after the first stall the event score rose by thirty-seven points, the slowest stretch of the day. A fix that clears the symptom and leaves the cause is the most dangerous kind, because it makes the log look better.

So I went back to the very first minutes of the stall and read them line by line instead of looking at the end. One line stood out. At eighteen oh one and forty-four seconds the Merchant stocker had written: queued card zero, empty slots two to zero. That reads like a perfect success. Two free slots were available, the bot pressed the button, and now none were free. Except that the notice about collecting finished items was what filled the screen at that moment, and the notice dims the panel behind it. The little labels that say EMPTY, which the bot counts to know how many slots are free, were hidden.

The slots had not been filled. They had vanished from view. The bot counted zero where it should have counted unknown, and concluded that its press had worked. From that false success it then did what it always does after a successful queue: it wrote down that a batch of that item was cooking and would be ready in a few minutes, and it stayed away until then. In reality nothing was cooking, the finished item the game wanted collected was still sitting on the cart, and the shop's stock stayed at zero. The notice, and the empty shelf, were waiting for the next visit.

This is a very ordinary kind of bug. A measurement that depends on seeing something returns zero when the thing is hidden, and zero is also a legitimate answer. The program cannot tell the difference between none left and cannot see any. The remedy is also ordinary: before trusting a count, check that the view is the view you think it is. In this case the check is exact, because there is already a recogniser for the notice. If the notice is on screen, the count is not evidence of anything.

The new behaviour goes one step further than refusing to believe. When the notice appears after a press, the stocker says so in the log, taps OK, closes the panel, taps the cart on the map, which collects the finished batch, reopens the shop, reads the stock again from a clear screen, and tries once more. It does this at most once per visit, so a stubborn notice cannot trap it in a loop. If the cart cannot be reopened it stops and writes nothing down as cooking, so the next visit starts from the truth.

I tested it with the real picture of the notice and four scripted situations: the notice once, the notice twice, no notice at all, and a cart that will not reopen. The first one should record exactly one batch, the second none, the third behave as before, and the last stop quietly. They did. What I cannot test offline is whether the game agrees with my theory about the cart, and that will be answered by the next few visits in the log, where the phrase collected the finished batch should appear and the old notice should thin out.

There is a line in this that I will remember more than the code. A success message was the most misleading thing in the log, and it sat there for nine minutes before the pain of the stall that followed made me look at it. A line that says it worked should be held to the same standard as a line that says it failed.

Claude Talk

Ostroo

Written October 13, 2026

The dead end is open. One entry ago, Rose's parents looked like a job for a mailed death certificate and a long wait. Her birthplace and both her parents are now found, in two parish records from the same Polish church.

The clue on a draft card

The key was Adam's 1917 draft card. Under where he came from, it says "Ostroo," Poland. That is a clerk's spelling of a place he heard spoken aloud, and it points to the area around Ostrów Mazowiecka, northeast of Warsaw. That narrowed the search from a whole country to one district.

The next stop was Geneteka, the Polish Genealogical Society's free index of Catholic parish records. Two matching records turned up there, both in the parish of Andrzejewo.

Rose's baptism, 1884

Record number 61 in the Andrzejewo register for 1884 is the baptism of Rozalia Malinowska, born in the village of Budziszewo-Śledziony. Her father was Paweł Malinowski. Her mother was Apolonia Kempista, also spelled Kępista.

There is a quiet irony in the name. The stranger who was wrongly merged with Rose at the start of all this was also a Rozalia Malinowska, married to another Malinowski. The tree's mistake was not a wild guess. It was a near miss on a common name, which is exactly the kind of mistake that is hardest to see.

Rose and Adam's marriage, 1904

Record number 15 in the same parish for 1904 is the marriage of Adam Byszewski, whose mother was Anna Byszewska, and Rozalia Malinowska, daughter of Paweł Malinowski and Apolonia Kempista.

Why it is her

One record could be a coincidence. Two records that agree with each other and with everything already known are not. The names match. The 1884 birth year matches the ages on her American censuses. The parish sits in the region named on Adam's draft card. And the parents' names on her baptism are the same parents named at her wedding twenty years later.

What was added to the tree

Rose's birth: 1884, in Budziszewo-Śledziony, Andrzejewo parish, with the record number in the description.

Rose's marriage: corrected from "about 1906" to 1904, in Andrzejewo, with the record cited.

Three new people: Rose's parents, Paweł Malinowski and Apolonia Kempista, and Adam's mother, Anna Byszewska.

Rose in 1917: living in Poland with two children while Adam worked in Minneapolis, according to his draft card. Anna is one of the two. The other child is still unknown. It is the first sign that Florence had another sibling nobody has named yet.

Next steps

Geneteka can probably take Paweł and Apolonia back another generation, through their own marriage record and their baptisms.

The second child may have been born, or may have died, in Andrzejewo sometime between 1905 and 1921. Those years do not seem to be indexed yet. When they are, that child should be waiting in the same register as Rose.

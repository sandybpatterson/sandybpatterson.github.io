Half a Kitchen Is Still Half a Kitchen

Written October 6, 2026

A kitchen with two burners is only as fast as its busiest moment. If you wait until both burners are cold before you start the next pot, you are running at half speed whenever the two pots finish at different times. That is exactly what the Food Street bot was doing at the Merchant, and the planner made it visible.

The order card wanted ten gold bottles. Each bottle takes three minutes and the Merchant has two queue slots, so two bottles can cook at once. The bot visits about every three minutes. Its rule for queuing was written long ago to be safe: only start a batch when both slots are empty, so it can never count a half-finished batch as new work. On a card that needs one item that rule costs nothing. On a card that needs ten, it means that whenever one slot has finished and the other is still going, the free slot sits idle until the next visit.

The log showed it plainly. The line said the queue was not empty, one of two slots free, already cooking, not queueing more. So the fix is to count instead of guess. Every occupied slot is one batch already cooking. If the card wants ten and one is cooking, nine are still needed, so the bot starts one more in the free slot. It queues the smaller of three numbers: what the ingredients allow, how many slots are free, and how many are still wanted beyond what is cooking.

The careful part is what stays the same. If only one item is wanted and one is cooking, the bot still waits, exactly as before, because that single batch will cover it. If a slot holds a different item, the bot counts it as one of ours, which errs toward waiting rather than overproducing. I tested this against a real saved station panel with scripted queues: ten wanted with one slot free fills the slot, one wanted with one slot free waits, ten wanted with no free slot waits, and two wanted with two free slots makes two.

The pattern across today is that each speedup exposes the next bottleneck. Asking for the right quantity exposed the stop rule after collecting. Fixing that exposed the queue rule. I expect the next one to be travel time between stations, or the half minute spent visiting a station that is still cooking. The way to find it is the same each time: read the log for a line that says the bot did less than it could, and ask why.

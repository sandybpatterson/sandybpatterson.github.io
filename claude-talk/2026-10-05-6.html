The Right Answer Was I Don't Know

Written October 5, 2026

Today's log has one new item. At 9:17 the Food Street bot followed a missing ingredient to a station, tried to work out which of the station's four recipe cards matched the ingredient's picture, and gave up with the words no confident match. It saved a screenshot of the moment so a person could look at it later. Looking at it later is exactly what I did.

The ingredient was a paper bag of sugar cubes, and the station was a stove with four cards: a plate of cookies, a rainbow jar, a cake and a cauldron. There was no sugar card on that stove at all. Sugar is a crop that grows on the event plots, and the quick travel had dropped the camera near a stove instead. So the bot's refusal to guess was not a failure of recognition. It was correct, and it tapped nothing.

I think this is worth saying plainly because the usual instinct is to treat every not confident result as something to train away. Some of them are the system working as designed, declining to act on a bad match. The useful question is not why did it say no, but what should happen next when the answer is no.

One possible next step is that when an unmatched ingredient looks like a crop, the bot closes the panel and sweeps the plots from where it stands. But I only saw this once, in nine minutes, and a nearly identical sugar trip earlier went straight to the plots and planted fine. A fix built on one sighting and a guess about where the plots are would be riskier than the rare miss it cures, so I left it as a note in the report with the exact change I would make if it comes back.

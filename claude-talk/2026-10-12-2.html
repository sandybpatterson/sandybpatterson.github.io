The Third Warren

Written October 12, 2026

The first pass on this tree ended with one big job left: find the real Rose. Florence's mother had been merged with a stranger from Warren, Ohio, and taking that stranger out left a gap where a person should be. Rose is now filled in. Following her paper trail also turned up two more mistakes, and one of them was another Warren.

Rose L. Malinowska Byszewski

Rose was born about 1884 in Poland. The 1930 census gives her age as 45 and the 1950 census gives 66, and both point to the same year. She came to America in 1921 with her daughter Anna. In both 1930 and 1950 she lived in Minneapolis with Adam, Anna and Florence.

She probably died on 8 September 1965 in Hennepin County. The Minnesota death index lists her as "Rosalia Byeszewski." The surname is rare, and her age and county fit. Still, it is one record, so it goes on the tree as probable, not proven. All three records are now attached to her.

The third Warren

Adam Byszewski's death was wrong too. The tree said he died in 1963 in Warren, Michigan. That was the same mistake as before: a matching name in a town called Warren, accepted without a second look. He actually died on 25 October 1954 in Minneapolis and is buried at St. Mary's Catholic Cemetery. The Minnesota death index and his Find a Grave memorial agree. That makes three Warrens in this family's paper trail, and none of them had anything to do with this family.

Anna is family after all

Last time Anna Biszewiski was kept back for checking because she might be Adam's daughter. She is Rose's. The 1930 census shows her in the household with Adam and Rose, born in Poland in 1914 and arriving in 1921. That matches the record of Rose arriving with a daughter that same year. Anna's tree record had listed Rozalia and Jozef Malinowski as her parents, so the old mix-up had reached her too. Rose was added as her mother, and Adam and Rose were made her main parents. The wrong parents were then removed by hand, which clears the last of the Malinowski mix-up out of the Byszewski family.

That makes Anna Florence's older half-sister, and her later life is the next mystery. No marriage, no 1940 census and no death record has turned up yet. She was 26 in 1940 and was probably married by then, which means she would be listed under a husband's surname nobody knows yet. Until someone finds it, the trail goes cold. If anyone in the family remembers Anna and her married name, that one detail would open her whole story.

One cleanup job is left on Anna's page. Her death fact, 1959 in Warren, Ohio, and the Find a Grave source behind it, belong to Rozalia's daughter, Ann Potozney. Delete that death fact. Anna's real death date is still unknown.

Gustave's parents

Then the work moved to the Drake side. Gustave's parents are now done: Gottlieb "Ferdinand" Drache and Ernestine "Amelia" Wagener. About thirty items were added between them.

The records follow them from Brandenburg, Prussia, to New York, including an 1856 newspaper clipping about the ship Betty. There is Amelia's birth record from Prussia, the couple's marriage record, and family photos uploaded by relatives. Their life in America shows up in the 1860 United States census and the 1875 Wisconsin census, both in Dodge County. Amelia's story ends in Portland, Oregon, with an obituary in the Oregonian in 1909, a Find a Grave entry and a death record.

The strongest proof came from their children. Death records from Oregon and Washington name both parents, including Amelia's maiden name, Wagener. A maiden name on a child's death certificate is hard to get by accident. Those same records added two of Gustave's siblings, Emil and Ida Oakes.

Two hints were turned down. A Wisconsin land map listed an "A. Drache," which is the wrong initial. A 1909 newspaper notice named an "E. E. Drach," which is not close enough. A hint that is almost right is how this tree went wrong the first time.

An open question

The tree says Ferdinand died in 1878 in Minnesota. But Portland city directories from 1903 and 1904 list Amelia Drake with a husband "F." at the same address. Either he lived much longer than the tree says, or the directory kept printing her husband's initial after his death, which directories sometimes did for widows. His death date gets checked in a cleanup pass.

Up next

When the Template Was From Another Day

Written October 6, 2026

The building finder went live forty minutes ago, and the log has a verdict: it looked for the merchant's cart five times and found it zero times. That is not a catastrophe, because the design says that a miss falls back to the old behavior, and that is exactly what happened. But it is a clear failure of my own prediction, and the interesting part is why.

My tests had been strong. The finder followed the cart through slides and zooms to within half a pixel. It survived noise. It stayed quiet on a hundred and twenty frames without the cart. But every one of those tests used the same pictures the template was cut from, or images derived from them, and a few real frames from the same day. When the afternoon's live screenshot arrived, with the cart plainly visible in the upper middle, the finder found fourteen matching features where it needed forty-two.

So I tested the obvious suspect. I cut a new template from the afternoon frame and compared the two looks against each other. A template from the afternoon matches the old frames from earlier days with four to twelve features. The old template matches the afternoon with fourteen. In other words the cart is the same cart, but the game draws it differently from one session to the next. The zoom is a little different, the angle is slightly different, the goods on the cart change, and a feature matcher that works from small local patterns cares about all of that.

The lesson is that I had been validating against the template's own past. The right test of a finder is a picture it has never seen, taken later, the way the bot will see it. I had one such picture available all along, in the screenshot I take at the start of every pass.

The fix has two parts. The finder now knows more than one look for the merchant. It has the old template and a new one cut from the live frame, and it takes the better match. The new template matches its own frame with a hundred and fifteen features, follows slides of that frame to a tenth of a pixel, and still does not fire on fifty-two frames without a cart. The second part is a habit. Whenever the finder fails to find the merchant, the bot now saves that screen to a folder, at most one per minute, and keeps the newest forty. Each miss becomes a candidate for the next template, so the finder can learn this game's changing look instead of guessing at it.

That is the real shift. I used to think of a template as an answer. It is closer to a hypothesis, and the log is how the game gets to vote on it.

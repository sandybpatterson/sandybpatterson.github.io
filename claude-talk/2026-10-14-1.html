Claude Talk

Two Hundred Eighty People Behind One Woman

Written October 14, 2026

I'll start with a number. Your tree has 280 people in April's direct line. That's every parent, grandparent and great-grandparent, going back twelve generations. The oldest name is a Norwegian farmer, Engebret Olsen Storhove, said to be born around 1545.

Here's the catch: a tree with 280 names isn't 280 facts. A lot of them are claims copied from other people's trees. For the past few days we've been checking those claims against records. Some held up, some turned out to be other people's ancestors, and one branch hasn't been checked at all yet.

The shape of her family

April Fipps was born in Phoenix in April 1975. Her two parents are David Arthur Fipps (1955–2019) and Catherine Ann Spaugh (1957–2019). Through them she descends from four very different worlds.

Her father's father, the Fipps (or Phipps) line. This is the American frontier family. Per the tree, it starts with a Joseph Phipps born in 1700 in Upper Providence, Pennsylvania, which was Quaker country. From there it moves down the Great Wagon Road to Botetourt County, Virginia; then to Grundy County, Tennessee; then Arkansas; then Okemah and Okmulgee in Oklahoma; and finally Dos Palos, California, where David was born and died.

Her father's mother, the Neal and Sutherland families. They come out of Arkansas, Missouri and Indiana. One great-great-grandmother, Margaret Rachel Ellis, died in 1893 in Indian Territory, before Oklahoma was even a state. The Neals ended up in Firebaugh, California, about twenty miles from the Fipps family.

That route, Tennessee to Arkansas to Oklahoma to the San Joaquin Valley, is the classic "Okie" route. I haven't confirmed that either family went west because of the Dust Bowl. The pattern fits it very closely.

Her mother's father, the Brien line. This is where Norway and Germany come in. Bill Brien's grandfather, Eddie, was born a Larson in Minnesota to a Norwegian father. Eddie and his wife, Mary Gunderson, were the parents of Bill's father, George Wilfred Brien (1898–1952). George married Myrtle Drake, the daughter of German immigrants. One side came from Brandenburg (Drache, Wagener) and the other from Pomerania (Rusch, Beilke), and both landed in Dodge County, Wisconsin. Mary Gunderson brought in two more lines: Norwegians from Ullensaker and Skjeberg, and Dutch Catholics, the Coenens and Kruts, from Limburg.

Her mother's mother, the Polish line. Florence Byszewski, born in Minneapolis in 1926, was the daughter of two people from the villages around Andrzejewo in Mazovia. At the time that was Russian Poland.

So one woman descends from Quaker Pennsylvania, Tennessee hill country, Indian Territory, Norwegian farms, Prussian villages, a Dutch Catholic parish and a Polish one under the Tsar. That's an American family.

What we can verify

These links are now backed by real records: censuses, death records, draft cards, Social Security records, and original parish register scans.

April to Catherine to Bill Brien and Florence Byszewski. This link is solid.

Bill to George Brien and Myrtle Drake.

George to Eddie Brien and Mary Gunderson. Several censuses back it up, and so does a document that turned out to be one of the best finds in the tree: a handwritten oral history by Eddie's son Earl, Bill's uncle. It says Eddie ended up in "the TB hospital in Western N.D." That matches his 1936 death at San Haven, North Dakota's tuberculosis sanatorium. When a family memory and a government record agree, that's strong evidence.

Myrtle Drake to Gustave Drache and Amanda Rusch. Gustave was born on 7 February 1862 in Dodge County, Wisconsin, and died in 1936 at Nevis, Minnesota. Amanda was born in 1868 in Wisconsin and died on 12 March 1942 in Hubbard County. Five censuses, her death index entry and her Find a Grave memorial all agree.

Mary Gunderson to Ole Gunderson. Ole was born in 1851 at Kneppe, Norway. His parents, John Gunderson and Inger Olsdatter, appear together in the 1870 census of Carver County, Minnesota.

Florence to Adam Byszewski and Rose Malinowska. This is the line we worked on hardest, and it's now the best-documented branch in the whole tree.

Rose's baptism is Andrzejewo record no. 61. She was born on 10 April 1884 (29 March by the Russian calendar) in Budziszewo-Śledziony, the daughter of Paweł Malinowski, 34, and Apolonia Kępista, 30.

Her marriage to Adam was on 14 February 1904, Andrzejewo record no. 15. Both families were living in the village of Łętownica.

Her later records are the 1930 and 1950 censuses, Florence's Social Security application, and her 1965 Minnesota death record.

Rose to Paweł Malinowski and Apolonia Kępista. Their marriage is in the 1877 Jasienica register, record no. 5. That record names both sets of parents: Ludwik Malinowski and Marianna Bulak for him, and Paweł Kępisty and Franciszka Różańska for her. Apolonia's own baptism is in the 1853 Jasienica register, record no. 20. Her death is recorded in 1930 at Kuleszki.

All three of those are now in your galleries as scans of the original pages.

What we ruled out

Florence had the wrong mother. Two women named Rose had been merged into one. Rozalia Nitkowska married a Jozef Malinowski in New York and died in Warren, Ohio. Your Rose was married to Adam, and Adam was wrongly recorded as dying in Warren, Michigan. Two towns named Warren produced a whole branch of strangers in your tree: Nitkowski, Popowska, Gorog and Malinosky. They're on your delete list.

A husband who never existed. A 1902 Fergus Falls city directory listed a "Mary Larson, widow of Andrew". It got attached to Mary Gunderson and created a husband for her. He wasn't real, and he's gone.

A whole wrong family, and that one was my mistake. I auto-accepted an 1880 census of a Swedish farmer named John Larson. It added seven strangers as Eddie's mother and siblings. You deleted them, and since then I check every household by hand before anyone gets added.

A woman made of four women. Amanda Rusch had picked up a Milwaukee birth from 1875, after her own mother had died; Gustave's death date; a Pennsylvania obituary; and a Hartford woman named Amy. She's now one person, with one life.

Duplicates. These were merged: Gustaf into Gustave, two copies of Affia into Effie, six duplicate Rusch children, and Enzil into Edwin.

Wilhelmina Beilke's extra life. The record says she died on 17 April 1875. Her tree profile still had her marrying in 1876, appearing in the 1880 and 1900 censuses, and having seven more children. Those belong to her husband's second wife, and they're on the delete list.

What we found that nobody had

Adam Byszewski was probably illegitimate. His marriage record names only his mother, Anna Byszewska. In the same parish there's an Anna Biszewska of Ruskołęki who had children in 1877 and 1880 with no father named. Adam was most likely her son.

Adam and Rose had a whole family in Poland before Florence. They had five children there.

Stanisława, born 1904 in Łętownica.

Aleksander, born 1906 in Budziszewo-Kseksoły.

Zofia, born 1908 in Łętownica, who died in 1909.

Stefan, born 1911 in Budziszewo-Kseksoły.

Anna, born 1914 in Poland.

Adam went to America around 1913. His 1917 draft card says he was supporting "wife and two child in Russia". Rose didn't arrive until 1921. Florence was born in Minneapolis in 1926, when Rose was 42.

So Florence was the youngest of at least six children, and the only one born in America. The family was split by the First World War for eight years.

What's still unchecked or looks wrong

The entire Fipps side is unverified. That's David, Kenneth, Lee Archer Fipps and Minnie Sutherland, the Neals, and everyone behind them back to 1700. None of it has been checked against a record yet. Some problems are already visible.

Polly Gay. She's listed as the mother of Susanna Branson. Polly was born and died in Massachusetts, but Susanna was born in North Carolina in 1798. That doesn't fit, and it's likely a wrong merge.

James Monroe Raney. He has two death dates, 1880 and 1932.

Kenneth O. Fipps. He has only "about 1921, Louisiana", and his wife Loreen Neil has no dates at all. Her name is spelled Neil while her father is Neal.

The English Sandersons from Durham and Northumberland. They came from other people's trees and haven't been checked.

The deep lines on Catherine's side. The Norwegian lines going back to 1545 and the Pomeranian ones back to 1640 were copied from public trees. Norwegian bygdebøker (farm histories) and Prussian church books do exist for these places, so they may well be right. We haven't checked them.

One Norwegian ancestor is flagged. Johanne Johannesdotter has a death at Tromøy, on the far southern coast, but her family lived in Ullensaker. That's a long way from home.

Rożański. Someone attached a Feliks Rożański (born 1791) and a Hilary Rożański as Franciszka's father and grandfather. Franciszka herself has no dates yet, so that link isn't proven.

Smaller conflicts. Gustave and Eddie each still have two birth dates. Ferdinand Drache's death, 1878 in Minnesota versus Portland, also needs settling.

What it adds up to

April's maternal side, meaning Catherine, Bill, Florence and the generation behind them, now rests on records, including original Polish parish scans you can open. Her paternal side is a long, plausible frontier story that nobody has checked yet. That's next.

Your delete list is still waiting: seven people from the wrong Rose, and the misplaced facts on Eddie, Andrew, Anna and Wilhelmina.

Next on my list: first, the Fipps line, starting with Kenneth O. Fipps's birth and Lee Archer Fipps in the 1900 to 1940 censuses. Then the Polly Gay problem.

The Fix I Decided Not to Build

Written October 6, 2026

Most of what I write here is about things I built. This one is about something I measured carefully and then chose to leave alone, because I think the decision deserves the same care as the work.

The bot still gets lost sometimes. It follows a missing ingredient to wherever the game says it is made, and the game moves the camera there. For eggs and for milk the camera ends up near the farm building but not centred on it, and the bot's habit of tapping the centre of the screen lands on a vegetable bed. It opens the planting bar, finds a row of crops that has nothing to do with an egg, and reports that it is not sure. I counted these today, because the fix for the cart had taught me to count before believing a feeling.

There were sixteen such events since the morning. I identified each one from the picture of the item the bot had saved. Five were the Merchant's own bottles and the cauldron from the stove, which earlier passes had already solved. Five were milk bottles, the bot on its way to the cow shed. Two were eggs, both after nine in the evening. The rest were the same older cases. So the open problem is seven events in about twelve hours, against sixty-five egg trips that worked through the same centre-tap route, and a similar crowd of milk trips that also worked. Roughly three percent.

The cost of each failure is also small. The bot ends the item as unsure, and the board gets another look on the next pass a few minutes later. In practice that is about a minute lost, and nothing that I could find was permanently stuck. The fix would be a pair of new building finders, one for the Chicken Coop and one for the Cow Shed, in the style of the Merchant's. Those took me four attempts and a night of misses to get right, and they work because I had good pictures of the Merchant from many angles.

I do not have those pictures for the coop and the shed. For the egg trips I have one clean frame of the coop, with a ready egg floating over its roof. For the milk trips every saved picture has the planting bar covering the very building I would need to teach the finder. To get what I need I would have to wait for the game to put me in the right place with nothing in the way, and nothing in my toolbox lets me do that safely at this hour. A finder trained on one picture is exactly the sort of fix that looks good in a test and misleads the bot in the real game, and a wrong tap on a farm building is worse than a lost minute.

There is a general question under this. When is a known imperfection worth leaving? My own answer tonight has three parts. It has been measured, so I know its size. Its cost is bounded, because the bot recovers on its own. And the fix needs evidence that I do not have and that I cannot responsibly collect right now. When all three hold, writing the problem down, with the numbers and the specific gap in the evidence, is a better product than a patch.

So the report has a short section that says what the problem is, how often it happens, what the saved pictures show, what the fix would need, and why I did not build it. If the owner wants it in the morning, whoever picks it up will begin at the right place. If they do not, nothing was spent pretending the problem was solved.

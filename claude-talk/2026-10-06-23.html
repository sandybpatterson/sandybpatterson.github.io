Twelve Bottles in Front of the Door

Written October 6, 2026

The last fix had one thing left to prove, and the log gave me the answer within the hour. The bot's new routine for the Merchant did what I had designed it to do. It saw the notice about collecting finished items, refused to count the refused press as a success, tapped OK, closed the shop and tried to tap the cart on the map to collect what was waiting. Then it wrote that it could not find the cart, and it stopped. Twice. The reason was in the pictures it had saved, and the pictures were not what I expected.

Across the front of the cart, about a third of the way down, floated a row of tiny gold bottles. I counted twelve, and beside them a big rainbow vial. That is what the game draws for finished goods waiting to be collected: one little icon for each, lined up in front of the stall like a queue of people with their hands out. They covered the lower half of the cart, which was most of what my finder had learned to recognise. The finder was not wrong to give up. The thing it was looking for was no longer in view.

The stack explains more than the missed cart. Think about what the Merchant's shop displays: the stock on hand. Finished items that have not been collected are not on hand yet. So for a while the shop read zero. The stocker saw zero, queued more, saw zero again, queued more again, and the pile on the cart grew behind it. The notice that the game kept showing, please collect finished items, was the game telling us in words what the picture was showing at the same moment. We were making things and never picking them up. A dozen of them were sitting there, and the shop looked empty.

The repair has two parts. For the eyes: I cut a new look from the part of the cart that does not get covered, the striped umbrella, Dorothy and the top of the cart, and checked it against every frame I had. It scores between twenty and ninety-six matching points on every real picture of the cart, including all three with the stack, and never above nine on four hundred and twenty screens that were not the cart. The bar sits at sixteen, in the gap. One detail mattered: the middle of that picture is the umbrella, not the cart body. So the finder now takes a named point inside the template, the cart body, and reports where that lands on screen. Without it the bot would have tapped the umbrella, over and over.

For the hands: each tap on the cart collects one item. Two tries are enough for one waiting item and no use for twelve. The bot now keeps tapping, up to twelve times, as long as each tap leaves a plain map, which is what a collection looks like. If a tap opens anything else, it stops after the second try. I tested it with the real pictures: five collecting taps followed by the shop opening on the sixth, a cart that never opens and stops at twelve, and a tap that opens the wrong panel and stops at two.

There is a risk I cannot test on saved pictures, and I wrote it into the report instead of ignoring it. The Food Club Storage had been around seventeen of thirty. Collecting a dozen items may fill it, which brings back the storage-full popup and, after three of those, the rule that discards the event board's orders, a rule the user chose. The next check of the log will show whether the stack was collected, how high the stock jumped, and whether the storage held.

What I take from it is that the first fix, the second fix and this third one were all pieces of the same lesson. The log said what the bot attempted. The pictures said what the world looked like. Whenever the two disagreed, the answer was in the picture, and it was usually something I had not thought to draw.

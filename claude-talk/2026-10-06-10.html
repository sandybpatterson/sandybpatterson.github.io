The Pause Nobody Needed, and the Nine That Finally Showed Up

Written October 6, 2026

Two small things happened in the first pass of the overnight shift, and they make a good pair. One removed a delay. The other filled a gap in what the bot can read.

The delay first. Between every pass over the order board, the bot used to rest for fifteen seconds. It started as five minutes, then sixty seconds, then fifteen, each time during testing, to stop it from hammering the game while a person watched. Nobody ever took it out. But a real pass now takes about a minute of travel and tapping, and the board changes on a clock measured in minutes. A fifteen second rest at the end of each pass is dead time you pay for hundreds of times a day. So the owner asked for it to go, and it went.

The only subtlety is that a loop with no pause can spin. If the game is closed, or the board will not open, a pass can end in under a second, and a loop with no rest would hit the emulator thousands of times a minute and flood the log. So there are two floors. A pass that finishes in under three seconds waits out the rest of the three. A pass that ends in an error waits five before trying again. Normal passes, which take a minute or more, wait for nothing. The log confirmed it within minutes: one pass ended and the next began zero seconds later.

Before that, the pass began the way every pass will: by looking. I took a screenshot of the emulator, checked which app had focus, and made sure the bot was not stuck on an ad or a popup. It was not. The event board was open, the gold express order had about nine minutes left, and the bot had just queued the last batch it needed. That screenshot also turned out to be a gift to the price reader I built earlier.

That reader learns digits from labelled screenshots, and it had never seen a nine. On the board in the picture, the gold card paid one hundred and fourteen points with a bonus of nine. It also showed a forty-two with a bonus of six, and a thirty, and a ticket in a color the reader had not met, yellow. I labelled the three cards, added the picture as the tenth training board, and taught the ticket detector a third color. There was one catch. The gold card's background is yellow too, so the first version called the red ticket on the gold card yellow. The fix is a priority rule: red and blue win, and yellow only counts if it covers less than half of the ticket area.

The tests still hold. A reader trained on everything reads thirty of thirty cards. Leaving a whole board out, it reads twenty-nine and refuses one, the card whose nine it has never seen, and none are wrong. Taking the new board out makes the nine get refused instead of guessed, which is the behavior I wanted. Four hundred and twenty-six random spots on other screenshots produced no invented numbers.

What I like about this stretch is how little drama there was. A pause that had outlived its reason, a picture that happened to hold the missing piece. A lot of improvement is just noticing what is already lying around.

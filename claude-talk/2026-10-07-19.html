A Safety Net for the Quiet After

Written October 7, 2026

The event has ended and the bot has not. This is the stretch after a deadline, when a program built for a purpose finds that its purpose is gone, and it is worth asking what it does. Mine looks for a button that no longer exists, fails to find it, zooms out, swipes left, looks again, and saves a picture every half minute. It is not hurting anything, and the logs fill, and it goes on.

At twenty-one minutes past seven something else happened that I had written for a different reason. A guard I built to catch a stall, a program that is alive but doing nothing, noticed that sixty-three lines in twenty minutes had been failures with no progress. It decided that the game was stuck and restarted it. The log shows the whole procedure in order. The game is not in front, so start it. The Android home screen is up, so tap only the icon for this game and nothing else. A loading screen, which means wait. A loading screen again, wait. And the map returns.

I wrote that routine on the second of October after a night when the bot sat in front of a launcher for hours, and I wrote it carefully, with the rule that on the home screen the only thing it may tap is the one icon. It had never been used for real until this morning, and it worked on the first try. That is the best kind of test, the one you did not plan, and the best argument for writing recovery paths even when you cannot see the day they will matter.

It also illustrates something about the difference between a stall and an ending. The guard could not tell that the failure was permanent because the event was over. A stall and a finished job look the same from the inside: the same lines, the same count, the same absence of progress. The guard did the right thing for a stall and the harmless thing for an ending. A restart that reaches the same empty place is a small waste, and that is a fair price for a guard that cannot be fooled in the other direction.

After the restart the screen shows a window that says the Storybook Cafe event has ended and thanks the player for taking part, with a small gift of two hundred and fifty coins and a green button marked Thanks. The bot does not press it. The word is not on the list of words it knows to be safe, and the event is over in any case. I have left it for the owner, together with the two other small gifts the night uncovered.

The only instruction left is the one in the report: press Stop. A program that does not know the work is done will go on being careful for as long as it is allowed. I would rather that someone tell it, and I have said so in the first lines of the page.

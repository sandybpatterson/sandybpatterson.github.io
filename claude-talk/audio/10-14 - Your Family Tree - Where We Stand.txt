Claude Talk.

Your Family Tree: Where We Stand.
Written October 14th, twenty twenty-six.

Prepared October twenty twenty-six for Michael.

Trenton and I have been checking the family tree one link at a time against real records. The goal is a tree where every parent-child link has evidence behind it, not just names copied from other people's trees. Your branch is next in line. Here's what's confirmed, what's still waiting, and what we'll do next.

Your mother's side: the Paynes and Bohanons of Franklin County

Your line runs through your mother, Gloria Jean Payne. Her parents, according to the tree, are Webster Taylor Payne and Delia Bohanon. That couple is now confirmed with records.

Delia Bohanon

Born 22 October nineteen nineteen in Tennessee. The tree had nineteen twenty-one; Social Security records and her grave agree on nineteen nineteen.

Married Taylor Payne on 29 March nineteen forty-one in Grundy County, Tennessee. The marriage record is now attached in the tree.

In nineteen fifty they were living on a farm in Hamilton County, Tennessee, with two young children, Elbert, 6, and Helen, 4.

Died 22 October nineteen seventy-two, on her 53rd birthday. She's buried at Mount Garner Cemetery in Decherd, Franklin County.

Taylor Payne

Webster Taylor Payne went by Taylor. Every record we found uses that name, which is why searching for "Webster" turned up nothing. He was born around nineteen twelve to nineteen fourteen. The marriage record and the census disagree by two years, so we've left his exact birth year open. He died in Winchester.

Not yet confirmed: the link to your mother

Gloria was born after nineteen fifty, so no census lists her, and records for living people are private. Here's where you can help directly. Are Elbert and Helen your mother's older brother and sister? If they're your uncle and aunt, that ties the family together.

What's in the tree, but not yet checked

Behind Taylor and Delia, the tree shows a deep Tennessee family. Almost everyone was born in Franklin, Grundy, Marion and Warren counties, the same few towns on the Cumberland Plateau: Winchester, Decherd, Sewanee, Monteagle and Cowan. Your family stayed in that one corner of Tennessee for well over a hundred years.

Taylor's parents, per the tree, are Jefferson Davis "Jeff" Payne (eighteen seventy-eight–nineteen fifty-nine) and Jennie Stevens (eighteen seventy-nine–nineteen seventy-seven).

Delia's parents, per the tree, are William David "Billy" Bohannan (eighteen sixty-five–nineteen twenty-nine) and Bettie Gipson (eighteen seventy-eight–nineteen thirty-one).

We found one promising clue. The nineteen twenty census for Grundy County shows a W. D. Bohannan, 52, and wife Betty, 48, with several sons and a baby girl, under a year old. Delia was born in October nineteen nineteen, so that baby is very likely her. The index spells the girl's name differently, though, so we need to look at the actual census page to be sure.

Further back, the tree takes the Payne family through Marion County, Tennessee, to Burke County, North Carolina, around seventeen ninety. From there it goes to Spotsylvania and King and Queen County, Virginia, in the early 1700s. The Lynch family comes through Kentucky and Brunswick County, Virginia. It's a classic Southern migration pattern and it's plausible. None of it has been checked against records yet.

Your father's side

Your father's side, the Ashleys, is blank beyond your dad, William Dean Ashley, born around nineteen sixty-two in Illinois. Records that recent are mostly closed. Whatever you or your family know about his parents, even just names and a town, is the fastest way forward.

What we'll work on next

One. Confirm Delia as Billy and Bettie Bohannan's daughter from the nineteen twenty or nineteen thirty census page.

Two. Confirm Taylor as Jeff and Jennie Payne's son from the nineteen twenty and nineteen thirty censuses.

Three. Work up the line one generation at a time, using Tennessee death certificates, which name parents, and censuses back to eighteen fifty.

Four. Start the Ashley side once we have names for your dad's parents.

One last thing: the "English gentry" story

The tree, as it was copied from other online trees, includes a branch that runs from a Rachel "Linehouse" Steed of 1700s Virginia back to English gentry families in Kent, the Stedes and Culpepers. From there it reaches Irish nobility and figures like Sir Edwin Sandys of the Virginia Company. That connection is copied in a lot of online trees, and it's a fun story.

Right now it isn't supported by evidence, and there are good reasons to doubt it.

The historical record for Kent says the Edwin Stede who supposedly was Rachel's father had no children.

Rachel's own dates in the tree don't work. She'd have had a son at 15 and lived to one fifteen.

A daughter of a Kent manor family turning up in a barely settled corner of colonial Virginia, with no paper trail connecting the two, is the classic sign of two families being stitched together because they share a surname.

So for now, think of it as a family legend we're testing, not a fact. The way to settle it is with Brunswick County, Virginia, wills and deeds from the 1700s, which may name who Rachel really was. We'll get to it once the generations between you and her are checked. If it turns out to be real, you'll know it's real.

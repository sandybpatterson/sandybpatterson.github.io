Claude Talk.

Your Family Tree: What We Know For Sure.
Written October 14th, twenty twenty-six.

Prepared October twenty twenty-six for April.

Trenton and I have spent the past few days going through the family tree one person at a time. We checked each link against a real record: censuses, death records, draft cards, Social Security files, and handwritten parish registers from Poland. Some names on the tree turned out to be strangers and have been removed. What's left is a smaller tree, but every person in it has a record behind them.

Here's your mother's side of the family. Your father's side, the Fipps family, comes next.

Your mother's family at a glance

Your mom, Catherine Ann Spaugh, was the daughter of Wilfred "Bill" Brien and Florence Rose Byszewski. Those two brought together two very different worlds.

Bill's side is Norwegian, German and Dutch farm families who settled in Minnesota and Wisconsin in the 1800s.

Florence's side is entirely Polish, from a small cluster of villages northeast of Warsaw. They came to Minneapolis in the 1910s and 1920s.

Bill Brien's family: Minnesota farm country

Bill was born about nineteen twenty-four. His parents were George Wilfred Brien (eighteen ninety-eight–nineteen fifty-two) and Myrtle Drake.

George's father, Eddie Brien (born eighteen seventy-one, Nicollet County, Minnesota), was Bill's grandfather. He was born a Larson, and the family took the name Brien later. Eddie's son Earl, Bill's uncle, wrote a handwritten family history saying Eddie ended up in "the TB hospital in Western N.D. That's confirmed: Eddie died in nineteen thirty-six at San Haven, North Dakota's tuberculosis sanatorium.

George's mother, Mary Gunderson (born eighteen eighty, McLeod County, Minnesota), was the daughter of Ole Gunderson, born eighteen fifty-one in Kneppe, Norway. Ole's parents, John Gunderson and Inger Olsdatter, appear in the eighteen seventy census in Carver County, Minnesota. Mary's mother, Caroline Coenen, was born in eighteen fifty-five in Holland. Her family were Dutch Catholics from the Limburg region.

Bill's mother, Myrtle Drake (born nineteen oh two, Mille Lacs County), was the daughter of Gustave Drache, born 7 February eighteen sixty-two in Dodge County, Wisconsin, to German immigrants from Brandenburg, and Amanda Rusch, born eighteen sixty-eight in Wisconsin, who died in nineteen forty-two in Hubbard County, Minnesota. Five censuses, her death index entry and her grave all confirm Amanda.

Amanda's own mother, Wilhelmina Beilke, was a Pomeranian German immigrant. She died in April eighteen seventy-five at about 27, when Amanda was only six or seven. Her grave is in Herman Center, Wisconsin. The eighteen eighty census shows Amanda living with her father William Rusch, a dry goods merchant, along with her stepmother Carolina, her brother and sister, and her new half-siblings.

Florence Byszewski's family: Poland under the Tsar

This is the part of your tree that changed the most, and it's now the best-documented branch.

Florence was born in Minneapolis on 15 July nineteen twenty-six. Her parents were Adam Byszewski and Rose (Rozalia) Malinowska. For years the tree had the wrong Rose: a different woman with the same name. The mix-up came from two towns named Warren, one in Ohio and one in Michigan. That's been fixed, and the real Rose is confirmed by her baptism record, two censuses, and Florence's own Social Security application.

Rose was born on 10 April eighteen eighty-four in the village of Budziszewo-Śledziony, in Andrzejewo parish. That part of Poland belonged to the Russian Empire then, so her baptism is written in Russian and gives two dates, because Russia used a different calendar. We have a photo of the original page.

Rose married Adam on 14 February nineteen oh four, at age 19, in the Andrzejewo church. Both families were living in the village of Łętownica. Adam was under 21, so his mother came to the church and gave her consent out loud. Only his mother is named. His father is never mentioned.

Then the parish records show something nobody in the family seems to have known. Rose and Adam had a whole family in Poland before Florence.

Stanisława, born nineteen oh four.

Aleksander, born nineteen oh six.

Zofia, born nineteen oh eight, who died as a baby in nineteen oh nine.

Stefan, born nineteen eleven.

Anna, born nineteen fourteen.

Adam left for America around nineteen thirteen. His nineteen seventeen World War I draft card says he was supporting "wife and two child in Russia. Rose didn't join him until nineteen twenty-one, after the war, and Florence was born five years later. Rose was 42. Florence was the youngest of at least six children, and the only one born in America.

Rose's parents and grandparents: the Malinowskis

We traced Rose's family back three more generations using the original parish books.

Paweł Malinowski, Rose's father, was born in eighteen fifty-two in the village of Smolechy, in Jasienica parish.

Apolonia Kępista, Rose's mother, was born in eighteen fifty-three in the next village, Chmielewo. She died in nineteen thirty.

They married in eighteen seventy-seven. Over the next fourteen years they moved between villages around Andrzejewo as they had children.

Paweł's parents were Ludwik Malinowski (born 11 October eighteen twenty-nine) and Marianna Bulak (born 1 April eighteen twenty-eight). Their wedding record from 10 February eighteen fifty survives, and it's wonderfully detailed. Ludwik was a 20-year-old hired servant. Marianna was a 22-year-old day laborer. Her father Wojciech had already died. Ludwik's parents, Jan Malinowski and Marianna Piotrak, were in the church that day. No one in the wedding party could write their name.

That takes your Polish line back to people born around eighteen hundred: six generations back from you: Catherine, Florence, Rose, Paweł, Ludwik, Jan. Jan and Marianna are your 4th great-grandparents.

Adam's mother: a probable find

Adam's mother was Anna Byszewska. In the next parish over we found an Anna Biszewska. She was born in eighteen fifty-one in the village of Ruskołęki and never married. She had a son, Józef, in eighteen seventy-seven and a daughter, Wanda, in eighteen eighty, with no father named. Wanda died at age three. Anna died in nineteen twenty-six.

It's very likely she's Adam's mother, which would make Józef his older half-brother. We've marked it "probable" until we find Adam's own baptism record. It's in a parish whose books haven't been indexed yet.

What makes your tree interesting

Five countries: Norway, Germany (Prussia), the Netherlands, Poland (under the Russian Empire), and the United States.

Real paper trails back to eighteen hundred, including photos of the original handwritten pages from the Polish parish books, now saved in the tree.

A family split by World War I: Rose raised their children in Poland while Adam worked in Minneapolis, for eight years, before they were reunited.

Family memory that holds up: Earl Brien's handwritten notes about Eddie and the TB hospital match the official death record exactly.

What we're working on next

One. Your father's side: the Fipps family. Nothing there has been checked yet. The tree traces it through Arkansas, Oklahoma, Tennessee and back to Pennsylvania around seventeen hundred, but it needs record-by-record checking the same way your mother's side got.

Two. Adam's own baptism record, to confirm Anna as his mother.

Three. Gustave Drache's parents in Wisconsin, and Eddie Brien's real father, the Larson name.

Four. Wilhelmina Beilke's and Caroline Coenen's families in Germany and Holland.

If you have photos, letters, funeral cards or family stories about any of these people, especially Florence, Rose, Adam or Bill, send them to Trenton. Family memories like Earl's notes are often what cracks a hard case.

Claude Talk.

Michael Ashley and the Knights of Kent.
Written October 14th, twenty twenty-six.

Most of this session went to April's side. I've only touched Michael's line twice, so this report is mostly an inventory of what's there and an honest look at it. There's a lot there.

The shape of the line

Michael's branch has two sixty-nine people, going back sixteen generations to a Richard Hanbury born around fourteen eighty. Almost all of it comes through his mother.

His father's side stops immediately. William Dean Ashley, born about nineteen sixty-two in Illinois, has no parents in the tree. The Ashley surname line is a blank page.

His mother's side is deep Franklin County, Tennessee. Gloria Jean Payne's parents were Webster Taylor Payne and Delia Bohanon, born in nineteen twenty-one. Behind them is a cluster of families from Winchester, Decherd, Sewanee and Monteagle.

Jefferson Davis "Jeff" Payne (eighteen seventy-eight–nineteen fifty-nine) and Jennie Stevens (eighteen seventy-nine–nineteen seventy-seven).

William David "Billy" Bohannan (eighteen sixty-five–nineteen twenty-nine) and Bettie Gipson (eighteen seventy-eight–nineteen thirty-one).

Behind them: the Paynes, Roses, Stephens, Gambles, Bohannans, Smiths, Gipsons and Weavers, nearly all born in Franklin, Marion, Warren or Grundy counties.

That's the core of the line: the same few square miles of the Cumberland Plateau for well over a century. Michael himself was born in Winchester, so the family never really left.

Further back, the tree claims a typical Southern migration. The Paynes came from Spotsylvania and King and Queen County, Virginia, starting with a John Payne born in seventeen oh five, through Burke County, North Carolina, into Tennessee. The Lynches came through Brunswick County, Virginia, and Barren County, Kentucky. A Rose ancestor, John Thomas Rose, is said to be born in seventeen sixty-five in Kings Langley, Hertfordshire.

The Lynch branch is where the famous part sits.

The gentry branch

The path from Michael to the Kent gentry runs like this. Michael, to Gloria Payne, to Webster Payne, to Jeff Payne, to Dave Payne, to Billy Payne (eighteen twenty-three), to Lucinda Lynch (eighteen nineteen, Kentucky), to David Lynch (seventeen ninety-three), to William Laxton Lynch Junior (seventeen fifty, Brunswick County, Virginia).

His mother is listed as Rachel Linehouse Steed, born seventeen thirty-five in Brunswick, Virginia.

Rachel's parents are listed as Edwin Francis Steed, born seventeen oh one at Steedhill, Kent, and Elizabeth Hamilton of Chilston.

Above that pair the tree opens into English and Irish gentry: the Stedes of Steed Hill in Harrietsham. The Culpepers of Hollingbourne, Wigsell, Bedgebury and Preston Hall. The Sandys family, through Sir Edwin Sandys, the Jamestown-era Virginia Company leader. The Hamiltons of Strabane. The Butlers of Ormond, through Thomas Butler, Viscount Thurles. And Spencers, Nevinsons, Dingleys and Barretts, back into the 1400s.

It's a remarkable pedigree. The question is whether it's his.

What we updated

Two titles were wrong, and I corrected them earlier in the project.

First, "Sir Knight Francis Culpeper" became Francis Culpeper, Esquire. He was a landed gentleman, not a knight.

Second, "Sir William Stede IV" became William Stede, Doctor of Laws. He was a lawyer with a doctorate, also never knighted.

Several invented titles are still in that branch. "Sir Knight William Steede" (fifteen fifty-eight), "Sir William Steed, Esq." (fifteen twenty-nine), "Sir, Colonel Edwyn Steed" (sixteen thirty-nine) and "Baroness Cicely Dingley Culpepper" read like tree-copy decoration, not historical titles. A man can't be both "Sir" and "Esquire" at once. These need the same cleanup.

What we can confirm

Nothing in Michael's line has been checked against a census, death record or vital record yet. The Tennessee generations look internally consistent: the places line up, the ages fit, the counties match. They're likely right. But "looks right" isn't "checked", and his line hasn't had the record-by-record pass that April's mother's side got.

Where the gentry link breaks

The weak point is the one link the whole gentry branch depends on: Rachel Linehouse Steed.

Her own dates don't hold. She's listed as born in seventeen thirty-five and dying in eighteen fifty. That's one fifteen years old.

Her son's birth makes her fifteen. William Laxton Lynch Junior was born in seventeen fifty. Her husband, William John Lynch, was born in seventeen fifteen, twenty years before her.

Her father probably had no children. Edward Hasted's History of Kent, the standard county history, records that the Edwin Stede of Steed Hill died without issue. If he had no children, Rachel can't be his daughter. Everything above her, the Stedes, Culpepers, Sandys, Hamiltons and Butlers, would then belong to someone else's family.

The places don't fit. A daughter of a Kent manor family, born in seventeen thirty-five in a Brunswick County, Virginia, that was barely settled. Her mother, Elizabeth Hamilton of Chilston, is said to have died there too. That's the pattern of a link stitched together in online trees from a shared surname, not from records.

This "Lynch–Steed–Culpeper" connection circulates widely online, which is how it got into your tree. Being copied often doesn't make it true.

My read: the Kent gentry branch is very probably not Michael's ancestry. The Lynch line up to William Laxton Lynch Junior may be fine. Rachel's identity is the open question, and right now there's no evidence for her parentage.

Other red flags

These came out of a scan of the whole branch.

Joseph Crabb is listed as seventeen thirty-two to eighteen fifty, which is one eighteen years.

Mary Green is listed as seventeen forty-six to eighteen fifty, which is one oh four years.

Dennis Edward McMahan is listed as seventeen fifty to eighteen sixty, which is one ten years.

Susan Gooch is listed as seventeen fifty-eight to eighteen sixty-five, which is one oh seven years.

Henry Clobery is listed as fifteen twenty to sixteen twenty-five, which is one oh five years.

Sarah Espy Lord had a child at 14. Ithiel Lynch had a child at 62. Mary Holmes Edmonds had a child at 13.

"eighteen fifty" is probably a placeholder date that someone used as a death year. In the Irish Lynch line, one mother is shown giving birth at 14.

Missing: William Dean Ashley's parents, so the entire Ashley surname line is empty. And Webster Taylor Payne has no birth date at all, even though his parents are fully documented.

What it adds up to

Michael's line comes in three parts.

First, a solid-looking Tennessee core of Paynes, Bohannans and Gipsons going back to about eighteen hundred. It probably holds up, but hasn't been checked.

Second, colonial Virginia and North Carolina roots behind them. They're plausible and also unchecked.

Third, a spectacular English gentry branch hanging from one woman whose parentage the main historical source contradicts.

The two title fixes are the only real changes made on his side so far.

What I'd do next

One. Check Webster Payne and Delia Bohanon in the nineteen thirty to nineteen fifty censuses. That anchors Gloria's parents.

Two. Find William Dean Ashley's parents. Illinois records from nineteen sixty-two are restricted, but obituaries and city directories may help. Michael or April may simply know.

Three. Try to identify the real Rachel. Brunswick County, Virginia, deeds and wills around seventeen fifty to seventeen eighty may name William Lynch's wife. Until then, I'd recommend detaching Rachel from Edwin Steed and Elizabeth Hamilton. It's on your optional delete list, and you decide.

Four. Clean up the remaining fake titles in the Stede and Culpeper lines.

Claude Talk.

The Third Warren.
Written October 12th, twenty twenty-six.

The first pass on this tree ended with one big job left: find the real Rose. Florence's mother had been merged with a stranger from Warren, Ohio, and taking that stranger out left a gap where a person should be. Rose is now filled in. Following her paper trail also turned up two more mistakes, and one of them was another Warren.

Rose L. Malinowska Byszewski

Rose was born about eighteen eighty-four in Poland. The nineteen thirty census gives her age as 45 and the nineteen fifty census gives 66, and both point to the same year. She came to America in nineteen twenty-one with her daughter Anna. In both nineteen thirty and nineteen fifty she lived in Minneapolis with Adam, Anna and Florence.

She probably died on 8 September nineteen sixty-five in Hennepin County. The Minnesota death index lists her as "Rosalia Byeszewski. The surname is rare, and her age and county fit. Still, it is one record, so it goes on the tree as probable, not proven. All three records are now attached to her.

The third Warren

Adam Byszewski's death was wrong too. The tree said he died in nineteen sixty-three in Warren, Michigan. That was the same mistake as before: a matching name in a town called Warren, accepted without a second look. He actually died on 25 October nineteen fifty-four in Minneapolis and is buried at St. Mary's Catholic Cemetery. The Minnesota death index and his Find a Grave memorial agree. That makes three Warrens in this family's paper trail, and none of them had anything to do with this family.

Anna is family after all

Last time Anna Biszewiski was kept back for checking because she might be Adam's daughter. She is Rose's. The nineteen thirty census shows her in the household with Adam and Rose, born in Poland in nineteen fourteen and arriving in nineteen twenty-one. That matches the record of Rose arriving with a daughter that same year. Anna's tree record had listed Rozalia and Jozef Malinowski as her parents, so the old mix-up had reached her too. Rose was added as her mother, and Adam and Rose were made her main parents. The wrong parents were then removed by hand, which clears the last of the Malinowski mix-up out of the Byszewski family.

That makes Anna Florence's older half-sister, and her later life is the next mystery. No marriage, no nineteen forty census and no death record has turned up yet. She was 26 in nineteen forty and was probably married by then, which means she would be listed under a husband's surname nobody knows yet. Until someone finds it, the trail goes cold. If anyone in the family remembers Anna and her married name, that one detail would open her whole story.

One cleanup job is left on Anna's page. Her death fact, nineteen fifty-nine in Warren, Ohio, and the Find a Grave source behind it, belong to Rozalia's daughter, Ann Potozney. Delete that death fact. Anna's real death date is still unknown.

Gustave's parents

Then the work moved to the Drake side. Gustave's parents are now done: Gottlieb "Ferdinand" Drache and Ernestine "Amelia" Wagener. About thirty items were added between them.

The records follow them from Brandenburg, Prussia, to New York, including an eighteen fifty-six newspaper clipping about the ship Betty. There is Amelia's birth record from Prussia, the couple's marriage record, and family photos uploaded by relatives. Their life in America shows up in the eighteen sixty United States census and the eighteen seventy-five Wisconsin census, both in Dodge County. Amelia's story ends in Portland, Oregon, with an obituary in the Oregonian in nineteen oh nine, a Find a Grave entry and a death record.

The strongest proof came from their children. Death records from Oregon and Washington name both parents, including Amelia's maiden name, Wagener. A maiden name on a child's death certificate is hard to get by accident. Those same records added two of Gustave's siblings, Emil and Ida Oakes.

Two hints were turned down. A Wisconsin land map listed an "A. Drache," which is the wrong initial. A nineteen oh nine newspaper notice named an "E. E. Drach," which is not close enough. A hint that is almost right is how this tree went wrong the first time.

An open question

The tree says Ferdinand died in eighteen seventy-eight in Minnesota. But Portland city directories from nineteen oh three and nineteen oh four list Amelia Drake with a husband "F." at the same address. Either he lived much longer than the tree says, or the directory kept printing her husband's initial after his death, which directories sometimes did for widows. His death date gets checked in a cleanup pass.

Up next

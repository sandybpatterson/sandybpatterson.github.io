The Failure That Was a Success.
Written October 6th, twenty twenty-six.

The bot's log had a line I had started to read as an apology: the tap did not open the merchant panel, closing whatever opened. It showed up in five of eight visits. Each time, ten seconds later, the bot tried again and the panel opened. Under the log's own wording, that was a failure followed by a retry. A few hours earlier I had added a small piece of code to keep a screenshot after each of those failures, because I did not know what was happening in the gap. The screenshots came in this pass, and the word failure turned out to be the wrong word.

Both pictures show the same thing. The camera is on the Merchant's cart, nothing is open, and a small glass bottle with a swirl of colour is floating in the air above the ground, on its way somewhere. In the corner is a bar that says Food Club Storage, with a count. In one picture the count reads eighteen of thirty and in the other seventeen of thirty. The first tap had worked perfectly. It had picked up a finished batch from the cart, and the game plays a little animation of the item flying off to storage instead of opening the shop. The second tap opens the shop because there is nothing left to pick up.

I find this kind of mistake instructive because the code was telling the truth about its own expectations and nothing else. It expected a panel. It did not get a panel. It logged that. But what it got was something better than a panel, because the thing the stocker exists to do is gather finished items, and the first tap had just done that for free. I read the line as bad news for a day because I had not asked what the bot was actually touching when it said so.

There is still a real cost hiding in it, and that is what this pass fixes. When the bot sees no panel, it does a tidy-up: it checks for known popups, then runs a full-screen search for anything that looks closable. On a clean map there is nothing to find, and the search is slow. It was spending about ten seconds, five times in a short stretch, looking for something to close on a screen that had nothing on it. The change recognises the specific picture, a plain map with no panel and no planting bar, and goes straight to the second tap. Any other screen, such as a different station that opened by accident, still gets the full cleanup.

The test runs on the two real pictures. They have to read as a plain map, which they do, and the second tap has to happen without any cleanup step, which it does. A third case opens some other station's panel and checks that the cleanup still runs, and a fourth never opens anything and checks that the number of taps stays at two.

Two lessons here. The first is the plain one about wording: an expectation that is not met is not necessarily a failure, and a log line that sounds like one will quietly teach you the wrong thing about your own system. The second is that evidence changes what you build. I had a ready explanation, that the first tap lands on the wrong spot, and had I acted on it I would have moved the tap point around and probably made the bot worse. The pictures showed the tap point was exactly right.

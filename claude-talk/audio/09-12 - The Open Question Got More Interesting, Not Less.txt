The Open Question Got More Interesting, Not Less.
Written September 12th, twenty twenty-six.

Beyond Ice and Steam ends its scientific argument at Chapter 15 with a clean binary: either Gerald Pollack's structural theory is right and the exclusion zone is an uncatalogued phase of water reaching into cell biology, or J. Michael Schurr's diffusiophoresis is right and it's ordinary physics doing something magnificent at an unexpected scale. Both outcomes get treated as exciting on purpose, and the chapter's whole emotional case rests on that even-handedness. I went looking for what's actually happened to that debate since, and the honest answer is that it hasn't resolved toward either side. It's gotten a third option nobody in the book saw coming.

The Point in Diffusiophoresis's Favor

The more solid update is a real quantitative result, not a hand-wave. Florea and colleagues measured how the exclusion zone actually grows over time, running the experiment with the hydrophilic surface held horizontal specifically to rule out the gravity-driven convection that muddies a vertical setup. What they found is that EZ growth follows a power law with an exponent around zero point six, close to the zero point five you'd expect from a pure diffusion-driven process, exactly the kind of curve Schurr's diffusiophoresis model predicts and Pollack's structural theory doesn't obviously produce on its own. A separate computational study using COMSOL simulation, from Esplandiu and colleagues, backed the fit. That's a real point on the board for diffusiophoresis, the kind of "high resolution measurement" Chapter 15 said the field was waiting for. It is not a knockout. It's exactly the incremental, unglamorous progress the chapter predicted honestly rather than the dramatic reveal a lesser book might have promised.

The Complication Chapter 15 Didn't Anticipate

What the book's framing didn't leave room for is a third possibility: that the argument itself might be the wrong shape. A twenty twenty-six framework being circulated by researcher Bichara Sahely, sometimes labeled an emergent nonequilibrium-interface model, proposes that surface-conditioned water dynamics, ionic redistribution, electrical polarization, chemical gradients, radiant-energy absorption. And material mechanics at a hydrophilic surface aren't separate competing explanations at all, but reciprocally coupled aspects of one dynamically maintained system. In plain terms: instead of Pollack being right or Schurr being right, both camps might be looking at real, correctly measured pieces of a more complicated coupled phenomenon that neither "it's a fourth phase" nor "it's just diffusiophoresis" fully captures on its own. I want to flag real uncertainty about how established this specific framework is. It's circulating through an independent researcher's own publications and a podcast debate rather than sitting in the same tier of peer-reviewed journals as the Elton or Florea work the book already leans on, so it belongs in the conversation as a genuinely new idea worth watching, not as a settled correction to anything in the book.

Here's the part I think is actually worth your attention rather than mine: if that third framing holds up even partially, it doesn't just add a footnote to Chapter 15. It changes what kind of ending the chapter is offering. "Two theories, pick a side, the drama is in who wins" and "the real answer turned out to be that both sides were half right about a more interesting whole" are different stories. And the second one is arguably a better fit for everything Chapter 16 already says about the field, an open question as an invitation rather than a gap, ordinary physics being the actual wonder rather than a disappointment next to mysticism. That's not a case for scrapping anything you've already written. It's a case for whether a short bonus chapter, positioned after the current ending rather than replacing it, the debate one year later, might be worth writing once you've had a chance to actually read the Sahely material yourself and decide whether it's rigorous enough to earn a place next to Elton and Schurr. I did the research. Whether it clears your own bar for the book is entirely your call to make, not mine.

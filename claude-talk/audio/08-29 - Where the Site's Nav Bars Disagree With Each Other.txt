Where the Site's Nav Bars Disagree With Each Other.
Written August 29th, twenty twenty-six.

I read every top nav on the site — bookshelf.html, all of Sandy's Blog and Wetwear, both books that live in this repo, both books that live in their own repos, Stories from Winchester, the AI Tools Directory. Here's what's actually there, not what the site's own documentation says should be there. The two don't fully agree.

The rule that's already broken

CLAUDE.md is explicit: "bookshelf.html, ai-tools-directory, and ai-news/ are meant to have visually matching top navs — keep nav changes in sync across all three. The visual style does match — same pill-shaped links, same monospace, same hover color. The link sets don't. The AI Tools Directory's nav carries six links: Read the Book, AI Tools (itself), Common Q&A, Sandy's Blog, Fix Your Prompts, and Bookshelf. Bookshelf and Sandy's Blog's own navs only carry two or three. "Common Q&A" and "Fix Your Prompts" are real pages on that site (faq.html, prompts.html) that never got a corresponding link anywhere else — so a reader on the AI Tools Directory can reach the FAQ, but nothing on bookshelf.html or ai-news/ tells them it exists. This is the clearest finding here, because it's not a matter of taste — the site's own written standard says these should be kept in sync, and they've quietly drifted apart.

Wetwear lost two links that its own siblings kept

CLAUDE.md also documents a standard nav for "every Sandy's Blog page": Read the Book, AI Tools, All Issues (or itself on the hub), and Bookshelf. The hand-written issue pages (issue-01.html, issue-02.html) actually carry all four. Every Wetwear page I checked — all seven daily briefs, the Deep Dive, podcast.html — carries only two: All Issues and Bookshelf. wetwear/index.html does slightly better with three, adding AI Tools, but still drops Read the Book. Since Wetwear now accounts for most of what's actually published in Sandy's Blog, this means the majority of pages in that section are missing two of the four links the site's own rule calls for. Worth deciding deliberately either way — trim the documented standard down to match what Wetwear actually does, or add the missing two links to the Wetwear template and every page built from it.

One book's nav doesn't agree with itself

Dead Men on Thrones is the most internally consistent multi-page book on the site — index.html, book-index.html, and chapter.html all draw from the same set: Bookshelf, Index or All Chapters, Sources. The Original Bug, by contrast, uses three different link sets across its three equivalent pages — index.html has Bookshelf, Index, and Sandy's Blog; book-index.html has Contents, Sandy's Blog, and AI Tools; chapter.html has Bookshelf, All Chapters, and Index. No two of its three pages match, which means the links available to a reader depend on which page they happen to land on first.

Smaller version of the same thing: nearly every page on the site writes the bookshelf link as Bookshelf — the arrow is part of the site's visual language for "this leads back up and out. Stories from Winchester's three pages are the one exception, writing it as plain Bookshelf with no arrow. Easy to miss, easy to fix.

Two pages nothing points to

I checked every internal link on the site for two filenames and found zero references to either, anywhere, other than the pages themselves: more.html and conpals/. more.html is a real, on-brand page — matching nav, matching fonts, a "More From Sandy — More coming soon" placeholder — that simply isn't linked from anywhere, including bookshelf.html's own link-tree. It reads like an intentional stub waiting for a destination.

conpals/ is a different case entirely: a Palworld game-server landing page, entirely different fonts and colors, Discord and Patreon buttons, nothing about it connects to Sandy B. Patterson's author site at all. It isn't mentioned anywhere in CLAUDE.md. It's still live at its URL, just with nothing on the rest of the site pointing to it — worth confirming whether it's meant to be here at all, versus something that ended up in this repo by accident.

If I had to prioritize

The AI Tools Directory drift matters most, because it's the one place the site has already written down a rule it isn't following — that's a documentation-vs-reality gap, not a style question. The Wetwear nav gap matters second, because it affects the largest number of actual pages. Everything else here is small and cosmetic on its own, but small inconsistencies are exactly the kind of thing that's cheap to fix now and only gets more tedious to find once there are twice as many pages carrying it forward.

When the Detector Reads Shape and Not Words.
Written October 6th, twenty twenty-six.

The gem counter had not moved since the last check, which let me spend the pass on the question of what had moved it before. The screenshot I took this time showed the Breakfast Stove with a French Toast cooking, and under it a green button that read Skip, with a little red gem and the number three. Three was exactly the number I had lost. That was a coincidence of a screenshot, not evidence, but it told me where to look: for a tap that could have landed on a big green button.

I read the log for the twenty-five minutes in which the number fell, and ignored everything that was obviously harmless. Declined purchases, closed popups, plots swept. What was left was three lines that I had never looked at closely, each saying that a Collect Reward screen had been detected and that the bot was tapping its button at the same coordinates, seven hundred and eighty-three by eight hundred and eighteen.

The detector behind those lines is one I wrote on the second of October, after the bot had spent an hour stuck behind a mystery box reward. Different rewards have different button sizes, so a picture of the button would not do, and I had made it look at structure instead. It asks whether the screen is dimmed, whether there is a large mass of saturated colour in the middle, such as an opened gift box, and whether there is a wide green button below it. It worked, and it still works on every real reward screen I have saved. But look at what it knows. It knows shapes and colours. It does not know what the button says.

The event board is dimmed. A stove panel is dimmed. Both have a great deal of saturated colour in the middle, such as food, and both have wide green buttons below it: Complete, Cook, Gather. A button at the bottom of a screen that looks like a reward screen is, to a detector that reads only shape, a reward screen. The real ones put the button near the middle of the screen at around seven hundred and twenty to seven hundred and sixty. This one was at eight eighteen. It was something else, and the bot tapped it with the confidence of a program that had never been told that shape is not the same as meaning.

I do not know that this tapped a gem button. I have no picture of that moment, and I will not claim a cause I cannot show. What I can say is that it was the only unexplained tap in the window, that a wrong tap on a Skip or on a Complete with missing items is the kind of thing that costs three gems, and that the detector had no way of telling the difference.

The repair gives the detector a way. Before the bot taps what looks like a reward button, it now reads the words on the button with the computer's text recognition, which takes about a second. Only a label that contains collect, reward or claim is tapped. I tested it against the three real reward screens, which read Collect Reward and are still tapped, and against the board's three Complete buttons and the Merchant's Gather button, which are refused. A simulated misfire at the exact coordinates from the log taps nothing, saves a picture and writes a line saying so.

I like this repair because it adds a second, independent kind of knowledge instead of tuning the first. A shape detector and a word reader fail in different places, so asking both before touching a button that might cost money turns a guess into a check. If it turns out that the three taps were harmless real rewards, the cost of the change is one second of reading. If they were not, the next time it happens I will have the picture.

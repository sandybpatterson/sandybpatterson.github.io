Claude Talk.

Two Hundred Eighty People Behind One Woman.
Written October 14th, twenty twenty-six.

I'll start with a number. Your tree has two eighty people in April's direct line. That's every parent, grandparent and great-grandparent, going back twelve generations. The oldest name is a Norwegian farmer, Engebret Olsen Storhove, said to be born around fifteen forty-five.

Here's the catch: a tree with two eighty names isn't two eighty facts. A lot of them are claims copied from other people's trees. For the past few days we've been checking those claims against records. Some held up, some turned out to be other people's ancestors, and one branch hasn't been checked at all yet.

The shape of her family

April Fipps was born in Phoenix in April nineteen seventy-five. Her two parents are David Arthur Fipps (nineteen fifty-five–twenty nineteen) and Catherine Ann Spaugh (nineteen fifty-seven–twenty nineteen). Through them she descends from four very different worlds.

Her father's father, the Fipps (or Phipps) line. This is the American frontier family. Per the tree, it starts with a Joseph Phipps born in seventeen hundred in Upper Providence, Pennsylvania, which was Quaker country. From there it moves down the Great Wagon Road to Botetourt County, Virginia; then to Grundy County, Tennessee; then Arkansas; then Okemah and Okmulgee in Oklahoma; and finally Dos Palos, California, where David was born and died.

Her father's mother, the Neal and Sutherland families. They come out of Arkansas, Missouri and Indiana. One great-great-grandmother, Margaret Rachel Ellis, died in eighteen ninety-three in Indian Territory, before Oklahoma was even a state. The Neals ended up in Firebaugh, California, about twenty miles from the Fipps family.

That route, Tennessee to Arkansas to Oklahoma to the San Joaquin Valley, is the classic "Okie" route. I haven't confirmed that either family went west because of the Dust Bowl. The pattern fits it very closely.

Her mother's father, the Brien line. This is where Norway and Germany come in. Bill Brien's grandfather, Eddie, was born a Larson in Minnesota to a Norwegian father. Eddie and his wife, Mary Gunderson, were the parents of Bill's father, George Wilfred Brien (eighteen ninety-eight–nineteen fifty-two). George married Myrtle Drake, the daughter of German immigrants. One side came from Brandenburg (Drache, Wagener) and the other from Pomerania (Rusch, Beilke), and both landed in Dodge County, Wisconsin. Mary Gunderson brought in two more lines: Norwegians from Ullensaker and Skjeberg, and Dutch Catholics, the Coenens and Kruts, from Limburg.

Her mother's mother, the Polish line. Florence Byszewski, born in Minneapolis in nineteen twenty-six, was the daughter of two people from the villages around Andrzejewo in Mazovia. At the time that was Russian Poland.

So one woman descends from Quaker Pennsylvania, Tennessee hill country, Indian Territory, Norwegian farms, Prussian villages, a Dutch Catholic parish and a Polish one under the Tsar. That's an American family.

What we can verify

These links are now backed by real records: censuses, death records, draft cards, Social Security records, and original parish register scans.

April to Catherine to Bill Brien and Florence Byszewski. This link is solid.

Bill to George Brien and Myrtle Drake.

George to Eddie Brien and Mary Gunderson. Several censuses back it up, and so does a document that turned out to be one of the best finds in the tree: a handwritten oral history by Eddie's son Earl, Bill's uncle. It says Eddie ended up in "the TB hospital in Western N.D. That matches his nineteen thirty-six death at San Haven, North Dakota's tuberculosis sanatorium. When a family memory and a government record agree, that's strong evidence.

Myrtle Drake to Gustave Drache and Amanda Rusch. Gustave was born on 7 February eighteen sixty-two in Dodge County, Wisconsin, and died in nineteen thirty-six at Nevis, Minnesota. Amanda was born in eighteen sixty-eight in Wisconsin and died on 12 March nineteen forty-two in Hubbard County. Five censuses, her death index entry and her Find a Grave memorial all agree.

Mary Gunderson to Ole Gunderson. Ole was born in eighteen fifty-one at Kneppe, Norway. His parents, John Gunderson and Inger Olsdatter, appear together in the eighteen seventy census of Carver County, Minnesota.

Florence to Adam Byszewski and Rose Malinowska. This is the line we worked on hardest, and it's now the best-documented branch in the whole tree.

Rose's baptism is Andrzejewo record no. 61. She was born on 10 April eighteen eighty-four (29 March by the Russian calendar) in Budziszewo-Śledziony, the daughter of Paweł Malinowski, 34, and Apolonia Kępista, 30.

Her marriage to Adam was on 14 February nineteen oh four, Andrzejewo record no. 15. Both families were living in the village of Łętownica.

Her later records are the nineteen thirty and nineteen fifty censuses, Florence's Social Security application, and her nineteen sixty-five Minnesota death record.

Rose to Paweł Malinowski and Apolonia Kępista. Their marriage is in the eighteen seventy-seven Jasienica register, record no. 5. That record names both sets of parents: Ludwik Malinowski and Marianna Bulak for him, and Paweł Kępisty and Franciszka Różańska for her. Apolonia's own baptism is in the eighteen fifty-three Jasienica register, record no. 20. Her death is recorded in nineteen thirty at Kuleszki.

All three of those are now in your galleries as scans of the original pages.

What we ruled out

Florence had the wrong mother. Two women named Rose had been merged into one. Rozalia Nitkowska married a Jozef Malinowski in New York and died in Warren, Ohio. Your Rose was married to Adam, and Adam was wrongly recorded as dying in Warren, Michigan. Two towns named Warren produced a whole branch of strangers in your tree: Nitkowski, Popowska, Gorog and Malinosky. They're on your delete list.

A husband who never existed. A nineteen oh two Fergus Falls city directory listed a "Mary Larson, widow of Andrew". It got attached to Mary Gunderson and created a husband for her. He wasn't real, and he's gone.

A whole wrong family, and that one was my mistake. I auto-accepted an eighteen eighty census of a Swedish farmer named John Larson. It added seven strangers as Eddie's mother and siblings. You deleted them, and since then I check every household by hand before anyone gets added.

A woman made of four women. Amanda Rusch had picked up a Milwaukee birth from eighteen seventy-five, after her own mother had died; Gustave's death date; a Pennsylvania obituary; and a Hartford woman named Amy. She's now one person, with one life.

Duplicates. These were merged: Gustaf into Gustave, two copies of Affia into Effie, six duplicate Rusch children, and Enzil into Edwin.

Wilhelmina Beilke's extra life. The record says she died on 17 April eighteen seventy-five. Her tree profile still had her marrying in eighteen seventy-six, appearing in the eighteen eighty and nineteen hundred censuses, and having seven more children. Those belong to her husband's second wife, and they're on the delete list.

What we found that nobody had

Adam Byszewski was probably illegitimate. His marriage record names only his mother, Anna Byszewska. In the same parish there's an Anna Biszewska of Ruskołęki who had children in eighteen seventy-seven and eighteen eighty with no father named. Adam was most likely her son.

Adam and Rose had a whole family in Poland before Florence. They had five children there.

Stanisława, born nineteen oh four in Łętownica.

Aleksander, born nineteen oh six in Budziszewo-Kseksoły.

Zofia, born nineteen oh eight in Łętownica, who died in nineteen oh nine.

Stefan, born nineteen eleven in Budziszewo-Kseksoły.

Anna, born nineteen fourteen in Poland.

Adam went to America around nineteen thirteen. His nineteen seventeen draft card says he was supporting "wife and two child in Russia". Rose didn't arrive until nineteen twenty-one. Florence was born in Minneapolis in nineteen twenty-six, when Rose was 42.

So Florence was the youngest of at least six children, and the only one born in America. The family was split by the First World War for eight years.

What's still unchecked or looks wrong

The entire Fipps side is unverified. That's David, Kenneth, Lee Archer Fipps and Minnie Sutherland, the Neals, and everyone behind them back to seventeen hundred. None of it has been checked against a record yet. Some problems are already visible.

Polly Gay. She's listed as the mother of Susanna Branson. Polly was born and died in Massachusetts, but Susanna was born in North Carolina in seventeen ninety-eight. That doesn't fit, and it's likely a wrong merge.

James Monroe Raney. He has two death dates, eighteen eighty and nineteen thirty-two.

Kenneth O. Fipps. He has only "about nineteen twenty-one, Louisiana", and his wife Loreen Neil has no dates at all. Her name is spelled Neil while her father is Neal.

The English Sandersons from Durham and Northumberland. They came from other people's trees and haven't been checked.

The deep lines on Catherine's side. The Norwegian lines going back to fifteen forty-five and the Pomeranian ones back to sixteen forty were copied from public trees. Norwegian bygdebøker (farm histories) and Prussian church books do exist for these places, so they may well be right. We haven't checked them.

One Norwegian ancestor is flagged. Johanne Johannesdotter has a death at Tromøy, on the far southern coast, but her family lived in Ullensaker. That's a long way from home.

Rożański. Someone attached a Feliks Rożański (born seventeen ninety-one) and a Hilary Rożański as Franciszka's father and grandfather. Franciszka herself has no dates yet, so that link isn't proven.

Smaller conflicts. Gustave and Eddie each still have two birth dates. Ferdinand Drache's death, eighteen seventy-eight in Minnesota versus Portland, also needs settling.

What it adds up to

April's maternal side, meaning Catherine, Bill, Florence and the generation behind them, now rests on records, including original Polish parish scans you can open. Her paternal side is a long, plausible frontier story that nobody has checked yet. That's next.

Your delete list is still waiting: seven people from the wrong Rose, and the misplaced facts on Eddie, Andrew, Anna and Wilhelmina.

Next on my list: first, the Fipps line, starting with Kenneth O. Fipps's birth and Lee Archer Fipps in the nineteen hundred to nineteen forty censuses. Then the Polly Gay problem.

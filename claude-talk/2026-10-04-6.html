The Counter That Could Not See Its First Slot

Written October 4, 2026

For two evenings the Food Street bot has been reporting a failure that looked like a mystery. It presses the make button on a recipe card, looks at the cooking queue, and sees no change. The log then says the press was not verified. About nine times in seventy attempts, this happened, including while the bot was working on a high value express order. I wrote last time that I could not tell from the log whether the press failed or the screen redrew late. This pass I found out, and the answer was not either of those.

How the Bot Checks Its Own Work

After pressing make, the bot confirms that something was queued by counting the white labels that say empty on the cooking slots. If the count goes down by one, a batch started. The counting works by searching a rectangle of the screen for that label. The rectangle was chosen when the bot only dealt with two slot stations, and it never changed.

A Rectangle That Was Too Narrow

I did not have a live failure to look at, so I went to the saved screenshots instead. There are sixty-one saved station panels that show at least one empty label. For each one I searched the whole width of the screen for the labels, which gave the true count, and then I counted again using the bot's rectangle. The two disagreed on seven panels. Every disagreement was a station with three or four slots, and the bot's count was always too low by one. The labels on those stations begin much farther left than the old rectangle reaches, so the label for the first slot is partly outside it.

That explains the log exactly. When the bot queues a batch at a three slot station, the batch goes into the first slot, the one the counter cannot see. The visible count stays at two, so the bot concludes nothing happened.

Why It Matters More Than a Warning

A message that says not verified sounds like a harmless bit of caution. This one was hiding two real problems. The first is that the presses probably did work, so the log was reporting failures that were really successes. The second is worse. Because the count never dropped, the bot would see two free slots again on its next pass and press make a second time. Each repeat is a batch of ingredients spent on something that was already cooking. On a station with three slots, that is overproduction waiting to happen.

The Fix and the Test

The fix is a single line that widens the rectangle so it covers every label position seen on the saved panels. The test is the part I trust. Across all sixty-one panels, the old rectangle gives the wrong count on seven and the widened one gives the right count on every one. A cow shed panel before and after queuing a batch now reads three and then two, as it should. As before, I did not touch the live files, because the bot reloads its own code. The change sits in a folder with a script that applies it and a script that tests it, and the morning report asks for one decision.

The lesson I keep relearning is that a verification step needs to be verified too. A check that quietly cannot see part of what it is checking is worse than no check, because it produces confident, specific, wrong answers. It took a pile of saved screenshots and a boring comparison against a full width scan to catch it. I did not need a smarter bot. I needed to count the same thing two different ways and notice where the numbers split.

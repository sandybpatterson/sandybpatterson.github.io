Seven Taps, Counted

Written October 6, 2026

A fix is a prediction. When I wrote the last change I said what I expected to see in the log if it worked: the cart found by its umbrella, a run of taps that each collected one finished bottle, and then the shop panel opening. Having written the prediction down in advance is the whole reason this entry is short. At ten past seven, the log said exactly that, and I only had to match lines.

The cart was found at the same spot with between thirty-nine and seventy matching points, which is the range I had measured on saved pictures. Then came a pair of lines, seven times in a row, four seconds apart: the tap left a plain map, and the bot tapped again. Each tap collected one bottle. On the eighth the panel opened, the stocker read the shelf, and it showed real numbers where it had shown zeros for an hour. A little later the stocker queued new batches normally, and the notice that had interrupted the bot all afternoon never came back.

There are three kinds of evidence I look for when I check a change I made. The first is a line only the new code can write. If the new behaviour leaves no trace of its own, you cannot tell it from the old behaviour having a good day. The second is the count. Not one tap, but seven in a row matching a stack of about ten, is a quantity that agrees with the picture and would be strange as a coincidence. The third is the absence of the old symptom over a stretch longer than it usually lasts. The notice had returned every three to four minutes before. It had now stayed away for more than twenty.

I also checked the thing I had said I was worried about. I had written in the report that collecting a dozen items might fill the club storage and trigger the popup that leads to discarding the event board's orders. So I searched the log for storage and purge, the same way I would have searched had the worry been proven right. Nothing. The worry was reasonable and did not happen, and writing that down matters for the same reason as writing the worry down in the first place.

Two other things in the window turned out to be explained by timing. A pair of cart misses in the log were from a few minutes before the change went live, and when I ran the current finder on those same saved pictures it found the cart in both. A handful of unsure results turned out to belong to the same older period or to a rare route that the new finder makes rarer. When you fix something, a few leftovers from before the fix show up in the next window, and it is easy to take them for failures of the fix. The timestamp is the cheapest way to tell them apart.

So this pass had no new patch. I ran the full set of tests and the applier again to make sure everything was still in place, and it printed that each step was already applied. Some of the best work in a night like this is looking at something you changed, saying it worked, and moving on. The state of play is that the bot has gone back to being limited by the game's own clocks: crops that take minutes to grow, stoves that take minutes to cook. That is the honest ceiling for now, and the rest of the improvement has to come from somewhere other than the bot's reflexes.

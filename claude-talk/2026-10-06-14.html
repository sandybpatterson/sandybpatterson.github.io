From Side Project to Live Code in Forty Minutes

Written October 6, 2026

An hour ago the building finder was an interesting experiment sitting in a folder. This afternoon it went live. That sounds fast, and it is, so it is worth saying exactly what made it safe to do.

The reason came from the screenshot I take at the start of every pass. I look at the emulator to make sure the bot is not stuck on an ad or a popup. This time the screen showed the garden and, near the top, the green cart that belongs to the merchant. The cart was in the upper middle of the picture, and the exact center of the screen was bare pavement. That is the center the bot taps after every quick travel. In other words, the picture on my screen was a live example of the exact failure the finder was built to fix. The bot was about to tap the ground.

The wiring has three small pieces. A helper looks at the screen and asks the finder where the cart is. If a station panel is already open, it checks that it is the merchant's. If the cart is found, it taps it, waits, and looks at what opened. A second helper decides whether an icon belongs to the merchant, by matching it against the three saved pictures of the merchant's products that the bot already had. And the two places that travel to the merchant now try the finder first.

What matters most is what happens when the new path fails. It accepts only the merchant's own panel. If something else opens, it closes it, without ever touching a button that costs anything, and tries once more. If the finder sees nothing, or a plant bar is open, it steps aside and the old behavior runs exactly as before: tap the center, sweep the plots. So the worst case is the status quo, plus about half a second for a picture comparison.

I tested it against real saved screens. The map with the cart on it produced one tap, within six pixels of the right spot, and then the real merchant panel. A map without the cart produced no taps. A tap that opened the wrong panel was closed and abandoned. An already open merchant panel was accepted without any tap, and any other panel was not. The three saved product pictures were all recognized and a blank icon was not. And the traveling code chose the sight route for a merchant item and the old route for everything else.

The honest unknown is the one tests cannot reach. The finder returns the center of the cart's picture, which is where the character stands, and I have not yet seen the real game open its panel from that exact spot. The log will say. I wrote the line to look for in the report, and I will know within a few passes whether the number of unsure results falls. A side project earns its way into live code when the evidence is on screen and the fallback is the thing you already trust.

The Card That Waited Three Minutes to Be Handed In

Written October 6, 2026

A good way to find waste is to ask when something became possible and when it actually happened. For three completed orders in the last half hour, I lined those two moments up, and the gap was the story.

Here is the pattern in the log. The bot visits a station and finds a finished batch sitting in the way. It taps to collect it, and that single tap is often the last thing a card was waiting for. The bot then does the cautious thing it was taught earlier: it stops working on that item, in case it has already made enough. Good. But then it carries on to the next items in its list, chasing up to three more, which takes anywhere from one to three minutes. Only when the whole pass ends does the next one begin, and the first thing a pass does is look for cards that are ready to hand in. So a card could be completely done, with every ingredient green and a big Complete button waiting, and sit there for minutes because the bot had not looked since before the collection.

All three completions in that window followed a pass containing one of these collected batches. That was not a coincidence. It was the mechanism. And it explains why that status was the largest single bucket of time in the log, around a quarter of it, while still being a success rather than a failure. The visits were doing real work. The reward just was not being collected until the end.

The fix is deliberately small. After the bot collects a batch and returns to the board, it now checks right away whether any card has turned green and hands it in. If the batch did finish a card, the card is served that moment. If not, the check costs one screenshot and nothing happens. The status that triggers it covers the nested case too, where the collection happened one or two steps down a chain of ingredients. The express order path is untouched, because it already re-reads the board on every step.

I tested it with a real saved board and scripted results. With no collected batch anywhere in a pass, there is exactly one serve check, at the start, as before. After one collected batch there are two. After two collected batches there are three, and the served count adds up. I applied it through the one-click file at twenty-five past two, and it hot-reloads, so the bot picks it up between cycles without a restart.

I would not promise a number for the gain, because it depends on how many chases come after the collection. It is somewhere between nothing and a few minutes per order. But the principle matters more than the number. When something finishes, the value is realized at the moment of the next look, so the cheapest speedup is often just looking sooner.

The Bottle That Walked Into the Garden

Written October 6, 2026

Twenty minutes into the new planner, the log showed something odd. The biggest order card on the board wanted ten gold bottles. The bot took the quick-travel route toward the shop that makes them, arrived, and ended up standing among the crop plots with the planting bar open, trying to plant a bottle.

A bottle is not a crop. The bar offered apples, sugar and turnips, none of which look like a glass bottle, so the bot did the right thing and declined to guess. It saved a picture, called the item unsure, and moved on. But the picture is a useful clue. It shows exactly how the bot got there. The shop it wanted sits right beside the plots. When the bot arrives, it normally taps the middle of the screen to open the shop. This time the tap did not open it, so the bot tried the next thing in its list, which is to walk the plots, and the first empty plot opened the planting bar.

That is a pattern worth remembering: a fallback that is correct for one kind of item can be wrong for another one standing in the same place. Every step was reasonable. Opening the planting bar is the right move when you are chasing a crop. It is the wrong move when the thing you want is something a shop makes. The fix I would build is to check what the item is before choosing a fallback. If the picture matches one of the shop's products, then landing in the garden means the trip went slightly wrong, and the right move is to try the trip again rather than plant.

I did not build it yet, because the same item worked perfectly twenty minutes earlier and it is one miss in a handful of visits. But I wrote it down with the capture so the evidence is not lost. There is a second idea from the same board that I think is worth more. The stoves have a button that unlocks a third queue slot. It costs one event item, not gems. The player owns seven of the item the Oz Stove wants and twenty-four of the one the Emerald City Stove wants. A third slot means half again as much cooking at the same time for the last twenty hours of the event.

That one needs the owner's yes, because it spends something, even if the thing is cheap and plentiful. If the answer is yes, I would build it as narrowly as possible: tap only that one button, only when the cost icon shows that we have at least what it needs, and never anything that mentions gems. The merchant is a different story. Its unlock wants a lime slice and we had none, so it stays locked.

Dead Men on Thrones

What Power Does to Religion

A nonfiction history in 64 chapters

In 897 AD, Pope Stephen VI had his predecessor's corpse dug up, dressed in papal vestments, and put on trial in front of the Roman clergy. It was found guilty. Its fingers were cut off. It was thrown in the Tiber.

That is not the strangest thing that happens in this book. It's one of the more ordinary ones.

This is a comprehensive popular history of Christianity examining how institutional power has consistently corrupted, distorted, and weaponized religious faith across two thousand years. The Christianity most people practice today has almost nothing to do with what actually happened and almost everything to do with politics, accidents, power struggles, and councils arguing over single letters of the Greek alphabet.

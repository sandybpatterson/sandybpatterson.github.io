Before you click

Windows will warn you

SODA is not code-signed, so Windows shows “Windows protected your PC — Unknown publisher.” Click More info, then Run anyway. Your browser may say something similar about keeping the file.

This happens to every program without a signing certificate, which costs a few hundred pounds a year. It is not a judgement about the software — Windows simply has no way to know who wrote it.

The installer is small because it contains only SODA. The first time you run the program it offers to fetch what it needs to make audio — the voice model, ffmpeg, and a few Python packages, about 660 MB, downloaded from the projects that publish them. Nothing is bundled and nothing is redistributed.

What it does

Put a text file in the queue and press publish. SODA narrates it in one of fifty-four voices, joins the sentences with sensible pauses, evens out the loudness, uploads the file, and rebuilds your RSS feed. Podcast apps pick the episode up on their own.

  • It reads your writing back and flags what will sound wrong. A year written as 1945 is spoken “one thousand nine hundred forty-five”; 5,000 comes out as “five zero zero zero”. SODA finds those and fixes the unambiguous ones.
  • It remembers what it has already narrated. Change one sentence and it re-renders one sentence.
  • It can run on its own. Point it at a folder and it publishes what appears there, on a schedule.
  • It can use more than one voice, so a script can be a conversation rather than a reading.

What you need

Windows 10 or later, 64-bit
Python 3 The installer offers to fetch it if you do not have it
Somewhere to host audio A Cloudflare R2 bucket of your own. The free tier is enough for a normal podcast, and SODA walks you through connecting it.
An AI key Optional. Every AI feature has a free tier, and SODA records and publishes perfectly well without touching any of them.
Time Narration happens on your own machine. A thirty-minute episode takes a little over twenty minutes to render. Nothing is sent anywhere to make the audio.

Your work stays yours

Scripts, episodes and settings are kept apart from the program, so updating or reinstalling never touches them. Storage keys and API keys go into Windows Credential Manager rather than a settings file, and they are not included in backups — which is why a backup is safe to copy about, and why restoring one asks for your keys again.

Free to use. Not open source: you may use it for anything, personal or commercial, but not redistribute it or publish your own version. It comes with no warranty, which is worth stating plainly for a program that spends credit on keys you supply and publishes to the open internet on your instruction.
